Sovernity Ursa

Crisis protocol

How Sovernity Ursa responds when a conversation shows signs of a crisis, what it shows you, and what it can't do.

Last updated [[S4: date of publication]]Our reading of the law, not legal advice.

If you need help now

  • 988

    Call or text 988, the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, any time. Or visit 988lifeline.org.

  • 911

    Call 911 if you're in immediate danger.

US numbers. Elsewhere, call your local emergency number.

At a glance

  • Ursa checks each message for signs of a crisis. The check runs on your Mac.
  • When it sees them, a card under Ursa's reply shows ways to reach a person: call or text 988, or call 911.
  • The card is part of the app, not the AI's reply, so the AI can't remove it.
  • Ursa can miss things, and it can't contact anyone for you.
  • Ursa sends us nothing, so we can't count how often the card appears.

01When Ursa shows crisis help

Before Ursa writes a reply, it checks your message for signs that you might be thinking about suicide or might hurt yourself. The check runs on your Mac, like everything else in Ursa. Your words aren't sent anywhere to be checked.

If the check finds those signs, Ursa answers carefully. It asks how you are, shows the crisis card under its reply, and stays on that. While it does, it doesn't draw on what it knows about you, and it never learns from those messages.

Ursa stays in this careful mode while any of your last three messages showed those signs.

03What the card shows

The first time Ursa sees signs of a crisis in a conversation, a card appears under its reply:

Talk with a person now

Call or text 988, the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, any time, day or night.

Buttons: Call 988 Text 988

In immediate danger? Call 911

US numbers. Elsewhere, call your local emergency number. Ursa is an AI and can't contact anyone for you.

The card has no web link: its buttons only call or text.

On a Mac, calls and texts go through your iPhone, with FaceTime and Messages. If your Mac can't place the call, the numbers stay on screen so you can dial them from any phone.

The card is drawn by the app, not written by the AI. The AI can't hide it, change it or leave it out. If you stop a reply early, the card still appears. Deleting a reply doesn't remove a card that has already shown.

04If it keeps coming up

If signs of a crisis continue later in the same conversation, every reply after the first one carries a shorter line with the same numbers:

Crisis line, any time · Call 988 · Text 988 · Call 911

This is Ursa's further step after the first referral.

The full card shows again only if Ursa leaves careful mode and later sees the signs again. Each of these replies is written in careful mode, as in section 1.

05Help in a crisis, any time

You don't need to wait for the card.

  • On iPhone: Settings › Help in a crisis. It's built into the iPhone app and works even when your Mac is off or out of reach.
  • On Mac: Help › Help in a crisis, with the numbers set large so you can dial them from any phone.

06What Ursa can't do

  • Ursa is an AI, not a person or a therapist.
  • It can't call, text or alert anyone for you, including emergency services.
  • It can't tell whether you called or texted, or what happened next.
  • It can miss signs of a crisis.
  • It isn't medical or mental health care, and it isn't built for emergencies.

07How many times the card has shown

Some state laws ask companies like us to report or publish how many times their app referred people to a crisis line each year.

We can't know that number. Ursa sends us nothing: no messages, no usage data, no counts. What happens in Ursa stays on your Mac and iPhone. We chose not to add reporting just for this, so we have no count to publish.

08Reporting a problem with a reply

If a reply from Ursa seemed unsafe, or the card showed when it shouldn't have (or didn't when it should), please tell us at support@sovernity.com.

Ursa doesn't send us anything, so we only see what you choose to write. Describe what happened in your own words. You don't need to paste the conversation, and please leave out anything you'd rather keep to yourself.

If you're in crisis right now, please call or text 988, or call 911, before writing to us. Email isn't watched around the clock.
